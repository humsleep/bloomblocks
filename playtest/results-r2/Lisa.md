## Out of character: tester notes

- **I solved every board analytically before moving.** I worked out each full solution from the ASCII (row and column runs, forced number sides). The wrong moves (J-1 C1, J-3 B3, J-8 D5, J-15 B2 and D5, W-1 F3/A1/F6, W-3 D6 and E6, W-8 D2/E2/G3/E5) were picked as plausible heuristic mistakes ("middle of the row", "obvious side of the number", "corner first"). They were not my genuine confusion.
- **All three hints (J-15, W-1, W-8) were my judgment of her impatience, not true dead ends for me.** I already knew the fix each time. A real Lisa might instead have quit W-1 outright, given how bad a level-1 failure feels. She also might have needed more hints on W-8, the 7×7 with 8 numbers.
- **The W-1 difficulty is a real signal.** It's forced but long-range (F2's "1" decides the top-right corner). Even analytically it took noticeably more steps than J-1, and five zeros give a casual player no positive starting move.
- **I noticed a "Dead cells" limitation on W-8.** It reported `none` while E5 could only be lit by breaking a number (the D5 "2"). A human reading "none" may conclude the position is fine.
- **The timestamps are not human** (about 30–60 s per level). Use the move, undo and hint counts.
- **I played ASCII with digits.** The phone version shows numbers as dot pips and zero as a ring, which I only judged from screenshots. A 44-year-old at night may count pips slower than reading digits. I also could not tell what the dashed and solid rings or the orange-vs-black pips mean, and I am guessing.
