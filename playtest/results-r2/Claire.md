# Playtest results, round 2: Claire

**Persona:** Claire, 36, Bristol (UK). Freelance graphic designer. Plays Monument Valley, Two Dots, Alto's Odyssey, A Little to the Left. Very sensitive to visual quality; deletes cheap-looking apps within a minute. Likes medium-difficulty puzzles with an "aha". Plays in the evening and in short gaps during the day.

Play order: J (1, 3, 8, 15), then W (1, 3, 8). Sessions: `playtest/sessions-r2/Claire/<code>-<level>.json`.
Both codes showed the same rule line as last week's V: "Light every cell. Lanterns can't see each other. A number on a rock = lanterns touching it." There was no lantern limit.

Counts are the harness counters. "Moves" counts every place or remove toggle. Taking a lantern off by tapping it again counts as a move, not an undo.

## Overall answers (in character)

**7. Compared with last week.**
- *Better:* The "dead cells" line is a big help. Last week my worst moment was all numbers "matched" with cells still dark and no idea why. Now it tells me which cell can't be reached any more, and I can usually trace that back. The hint naming every lantern to remove at once also worked well on W-8. And the new look (see 11) is a different world.
- *Same:* The rule, and the best moments: an edge "3" or a middle "4" you just know, then everything cascading. The ticked 0-rocks before you've done anything still look odd, though I'm used to them now.
- *Worse:* J's last level was no harder than its level 8. Last week V-15 had that five-step chain and felt like a finale. J-15 didn't. W-1, on the other hand, is too dense for a first level: zeros only tell you where not to go, which is a cold welcome. And a ticked number can still hide the real mistake (W-8, D2).

**8. Tomorrow morning, 10 minutes: one choice.**
**Version W.** It's the one with an actual "oh!" in it: the "4" on level 3, and the hint on level 8 making me see the "2" differently. One W board fits a coffee. Not J: I enjoyed it, but it's the version I'd play half-asleep on the sofa at 11pm, not the one I'd choose to wake my brain up. Not Wordle: done in two minutes. Not Royal Match: loud and endless, not me. Not "don't touch the phone": a quiet logic puzzle beats the news. If W's level 1 greeted me every morning with four zeros, though, I might swap to J.

**9. What would make me delete it now?**
Honestly, after seeing the new look, much less would. Ads over that beautiful scene, or a hint that needs a video. Levels that stay as gentle as J-15 forever, so it becomes tapping. Or a sudden spike like W-8 on a busy day with no hint available. And if the town turns out to be the same house reskinned again and again.

**10. To a friend, one sentence.**
"It's a gorgeous little night-time logic puzzle where you place lanterns so every tile glows and the numbered stones tell you how many lanterns sit next to them, and each solve lights up the windows of a house on a hill."

**11. The new phone look.**
What I notice first is the house. The moon, the purple dusk, the chimney smoke and the washing line fill the space that was an empty navy hole last week, and the windows lighting up as I solve is exactly what I asked for. The lit tiles are now a warm amber with a soft glow and little flowers, and the lanterns themselves are lovely. The brownish mustard is gone. This looks like something that could sit next to Alto on my home screen.

Things that are hard to see or understand:
- **The numbers are now dots on white pebbles.** It's pretty, and I can count 1–4, but at a glance the white pebbles with black spots read as dice or a Dalmatian. Worse, every numbered pebble has a little green moss blob on its edge, about the size of a pip. On the small SE screen I genuinely had to check whether the "3" was 3 or 4.
- **The zero is a hollow ring,** which I'd first read as a letter "O" or a target. Sometimes it's black and sometimes gold (gold on the mid-game screen, black on the hint screen, even though neither has a lantern next to it), and I couldn't tell what the difference meant.
- **Satisfied pips turn amber.** That's a lovely touch once you realise it, but on the mid-game shot the "1" on the left has gone dark grey with a white dot and a white ring around it. I couldn't tell if that meant "selected" or "wrong". (It has two lanterns next to it, so I think it's the "too many" warning.) Last week's red was clear, if harsh. This is so gentle I'd miss it.
- **Two shades of lit tile:** pale cream and deeper amber. I think pale means lit by two lanterns, but nothing explains it, and as a designer I'd ask whether it's meant to be information or just mood.
- **The hint screens:** "place" (a dashed ghost lantern plus a gold ring on the clue stone) is clear and beautiful. "Remove" is much subtler. The lanterns to take away get a faint pale halo, the button changes to a lantern with an up-arrow, and two stones get dashed circles. I'd probably miss which lanterns it meant.
- **The top-left squiggle-with-dots button:** I don't know what it does. Undo? Restart? A path? The "?" lantern on the right I'd guess is hint.
- **The tiny grey number at the top right** (1, 3, 6) is almost invisible, and I don't know if it's the level or a streak.
- **Completion:** all the windows glowing, the round attic window lighting, sparkles. Lovely, much better than "Garden lit!". I'd still love the whole board to bloom a little more, but it's a real moment now.
- **SE 375:** It fits fine, the tiles are still a comfortable tap size at 6×6, and the smaller house shows more of the town behind it, which I actually prefer.

Would I keep it? Yes. This is the first version I'd keep on my phone without apologising for it. My notes for the designer would be: make the pips unmistakable (lose the pip-sized moss, or move it), make the zero obviously a zero, make the "too many" warning visible, and label the left button.

**12. Day 7, and sharing.**
What brings me back on day 7 is the town. If I can see my street is fuller than on day 1, with more houses lit on the hill, a new rooftop, and some little detail I unlocked, that's the reason. The SE screenshot, with lit windows in the houses behind, already hints at this and I want more of it. The daily should be a W-style board with a proper "aha", a bit harder at the weekend. I'd share the spoiler-free result with my sister. We already swap Wordle grids in our chat, and I'd probably share it with my two designer friends too, because they'd appreciate how it looks. The streak matters less to me than the town, and I'd stop if missing one day wiped out my lit-up houses.

## Out of character: tester notes

- **Timing isn't human.** The session timestamps (30–60 s of active play per level) reflect command round-trips. A real Claire would take several minutes on J-8, W-3 and W-8, and might put W-8 down rather than press hint.
- **I solved each board analytically before playing,** then played moves I think she would plausibly make first. The wrong moves (C1 on J-1, B5 on J-3, D5 on J-8, B2 on J-15, F3/A1 on W-1, D6/E6 on W-3, E2/D2/G5/G3 on W-8) are my judgement of natural human mistakes ("central" or "corner" instinct), not genuine errors. Undo, swap and hint counts are therefore partly authored.
- **The W-8 hint was a deliberate choice.** I knew the fix, but judged she would be whack-a-moling at 36/37 and press hint, as she did on V-8 last week. A real player might restart or quit instead.
- **The "Dead cells" line makes the text harness more forgiving than a phone may be.** I don't know if the phone shows dead cells. If it doesn't, J-3, J-8, W-1 and W-3 would each have taken longer and might have needed hints. Also, dead cells seem to ignore number constraints (E5 on W-8 wasn't flagged while D2 was down, though only a number-breaking lantern could light it), which a human wouldn't know.
- **The J vs W difference** I noticed: J used 5×5 and 6×6 boards and its level 15 was no harder than 8, while W used 6×6 and 7×7 boards and more numbers. This rests on 4 and 3 levels.
- **Visual judgements come from six static screenshots.** I didn't see animation, sound or haptics, which matter a lot to this persona. My readings of the pip colours, the grey "too many" stone, and the pale versus amber tiles are guesses from the images, not confirmed.
