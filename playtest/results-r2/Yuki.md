**6. W briefly (1–5).**
- **What I did:** On each level I placed the big numbers first (the 3, the 4), then used the 0s, then thought about lonely cells that only one place can light. I made 1–2 wrong tries per level, and the board showed dead cells each time. I used one hint on level 3 and one on level 8.
- **Not sure:** On level 1, right at the start, I didn't know where to begin because there were only 0s and one 1. Then at the bottom of level 3 and the middle of level 8.
- **Hint:** Both hints said "Remove lanterns at: …" (E6, then D2). Each named exactly one lantern. After removing it I could see the right move within a few seconds. On level 8 the hint surprised me, because D2 sat next to a number that was ✓, and the problem showed up at a different number (the lower 2). The hint tells me what is wrong but not why. I worked out the why myself afterwards, and that was OK.
- **Stuck and couldn't tell why:** Level 3, F5. The board called it "dead", but in my head a lantern could go there. The reason was the number, not the light, and that took a moment. Level 8 was similar: the ⊘ appeared at the lower 2, but the lantern to move was near the upper 2.
- **Last vs first:** Harder, clearly. Level 8 was 7×7 with many numbers that depend on each other. But level 1 was already harder than J's level 15.

## After both versions

**7. Compared with last week (my notes).**
- **Same:** J feels almost exactly like last week's V: number-led Akari, calm, every lantern has a reason. The "2 or 3 in a corner gives sure lanterns" opening again on levels 1 and 3.
- **Better:** The new look (see 11) fixes my biggest complaint: rocks are now clearly rocks. W gives the deeper Nikoli feeling I liked in V level 15, maybe even more (level 8 had a real "only C1 can light C3" moment). The hint is useful. - **Worse:** J's last level is easier than last week's last level, so J doesn't grow. W starts too hard: a 6×6 board full of 0s as level 1 would scare my daughter. In W I needed hints. On a crowded train that is fine, but it is a little humbling.

**8. Tomorrow, 10 minutes: one choice.**
**Version W.** Ten minutes is enough for one or two W boards, and W gives me the "aha" that makes me feel clever at my station. I can use one hint if the train is crowded and I lose my thread. Not J: it is calm and nice, but I already know where every lantern goes, so I'd finish level 15 feeling nothing special. (J would be my choice before bed when I'm tired.) Not Wordle: English words. Not Royal Match: loud, ads, pressure. Not "don't touch the phone": a good puzzle is my small luxury.

**9. What would make me delete it now?**
Ads between levels, timers, or pop-ups asking me to buy hints. Also, if the hint said only English sentences with no picture. And if every level were like J's last level, all forced, I'd get bored after a week.

## Out of character: tester notes

- I played from the ASCII board and worked out the deductions as text, with cell names. A real person on a phone tracks sightlines visually and is slower on 6×6 and 7×7 boards. The timestamps in the session files do not reflect real thinking time, because I usually worked out several moves before running the commands, and the "seconds since start" shown later includes time I spent elsewhere.
- I chose my mistakes to be plausible first tries for a human: J-8 D5, W-1 E2, W-3 D6 and E6, W-8 D1, E2 and D2. In most cases I had already seen that they would fail before placing them, except that I had not fully checked W-8 D2/E3 ahead of time. I used the two hints on W where I judged a tired commuter would. A real Yuki might solve W-3 without a hint, or need more on W-8.
- The harness shows English status text ("Dead cells", "Numbers", ✓/↑/⊘). I used those marks heavily to notice mistakes. The phone app shows no such text, so a real player's feedback there depends on how the app shows dead cells and over-full numbers (the screenshots show gold pips and dashed rings, but no dead-cell marker I could see). This may make W noticeably harder on the phone than it was for me.
- My judgment of which English she understands, and my comments on the screenshots (colors, the meaning of the icons), are estimates. I can read everything, and I see static PNGs, not a live device with brightness or glare.
- I did not open any file other than the ones allowed. The project's CLAUDE.md was shown to me automatically by the environment. I did not use it.
