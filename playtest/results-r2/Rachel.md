### After J (in character)

1. **What I did, in order.** Level 1: corner "2", then the middle "2", then the two loose corners. Level 3: the right-edge "2" next to the "0", then the "3", then the "1", then the top-left. 2. **Unsure moment?** Only at the bottom of level 3, and that was me being lazy, not the puzzle being unclear. I tapped B5 instead of thinking it through. Otherwise I always knew the next move.
3. **Hints?** None. I don't use hints on a timed puzzle. It's like looking at the answer key.
4. **Stuck without knowing why?** No. When I was wrong, the "dead cells: A4" line told me exactly why.

Last week's level 15 was 7×7 and needed a real either-or. This felt like the difficulty stopped growing halfway. By level 15 I had the whole recipe: take the edge number, take the forced lanterns, mop up the corners. That's exactly when I get bored.

W's level 1 is the opposite problem, a hard, all-zeros opener. Nothing is worse than last week's C or S; those are gone, thank goodness. Still no timer that I can see anywhere.
8. **Tomorrow, 10 minutes, one choice: Version W.** It's the one that still made me think on level 8, and it grows the way Queens does over the week. Not J because I had its whole recipe by level 8, and I'd be bored by Thursday. Not Wordle, which I do on the bus anyway. Not Royal Match, which has lives and boosters and would eat my morning. 9. **What would make me delete it now.** More than one right answer (times would mean nothing), lives or ads, a lost streak because of a sync bug, no visible time to compare, or every board settling into one recipe like J did.
10. **To a friend, one sentence.** "It's like Queens with lanterns: light every square of the garden without two lanterns seeing each other, and the dots on the rocks tell you how many lanterns touch them."
11. **Screenshots (new phone look).** First thing I notice: it's *so* much prettier. The lit squares now actually glow yellow-gold instead of last week's muddy mustard, and the house lights up a window as you go. That's charming. The plain grey rocks and the white numbered rocks are easy to tell apart now, which was my big squint complaint last week. *Hard to see or understand:*
    - The numbers are now dots, like on dice, and a "0" is a ring. I can read them without my glasses, which is good. But I count dots slower than I read a digit, and on a speed puzzle that matters. A "4" would be a little cluster to count.
    - The dots turn orange when the rock is satisfied. I worked that out only by comparing pictures. In one shot (02) a rock is darker with a white ring around it, and I think that means "too many", but nobody tells me that.
    - Two shades of lit square, bright yellow and orange. Is one lit twice? No idea.
    - The hint screenshots (03, 04): dashed circles around some rocks, a dashed ghost lantern, and a lantern icon with an up-arrow on the hint button. I'd guess "lift these" and "put one here", but it's guesswork.
    - The left button (a squiggle) means nothing to me. Undo? Restart?
    - The level number is a tiny grey "3" in the top right corner, and there is **still no timer**.
    - The top half is still all sky and house, but at least now it does something. On the small phone (08) it's a better balance.

    *Would I keep it?* Yes, if there's a timer, or at least a time in the share text, and the boards don't go flat like J.

The house windows are cute, I'll admit I'd like to see the whole town lit, but that's a bonus, not the reason. ## Out of character: tester notes

- I solved every board by deduction from the ASCII grid before tapping. My few "wrong tries" were real reasoning shortcuts on my part (J-3 B5, W-3 E6, W-8 D1, where I genuinely missed that C3 needs C1). I then played them on the board because a speed-focused human would tap instead of finishing the check in her head. A real player would probably make more wrong taps on W-1 and W-8, and might well use a hint on W-1.
- I never used a hint, so I can't report what the hint text says in-game. My reading of the hint screenshots is a guess from the images only.
- The times come from wall-clock timestamps that include my reasoning and shell round-trips. They are not human tap speed. Relative order (W-8 slowest; J-8 and J-15 about equal) is probably meaningful, but the absolute values are not.
- Reading coordinates and whole rows or columns at once on ASCII is easier than on a phone, which flatters my speed. On the other hand, the digits were faster for me to read than the dice-pip rocks in the screenshots would be.
- I didn't verify uniqueness exhaustively. Each board I solved had every step forced in my own deduction, so I believe they are unique.
- The judgments about colour and state meaning (orange pips = satisfied, dark rock with a ring = too many, two shades of lit floor) are inferred from the static screenshots and may be wrong.
- The project's CLAUDE.md was loaded into my context automatically by the harness. I did not open any other files, and I did not use its contents for my answers.
