**1. Did the J levels get harder as I went? Where?**
Yes, this time they really grew. Level 12 (6×6) was a gentle warm-up. Level 16 (7×7) was a bit bigger, with one corner trap. Level 20 felt harder in a different way: few numbers, so I had to find lonely pockets and watch long rows. Level 30 (8×8, 13 lanterns) was clearly the biggest and the deepest. It is the first time the "last level" felt like a real last level. Levels 12 and 16 felt about the same difficulty. The real step up came at 20 and 30.

**2. Stuck and couldn't tell why? Did the "Stuck:" line point to the right thing?**
Mostly yes, and better than last week.
- J-12: "C6 (light from B4 A5; rock C5)". The rock C5 (my 1 already had its lantern) was the real point. Clear enough.
- J-30: "C3 (light from E2; rocks …)". It named my wrong lantern E2. This is exactly what I wanted last week.
- J-16: "C2 (light from A1 F3 D4 B6 E6; rocks G1 C3 G3)". This one was too long. It names five lanterns and three rocks. A1 was first in the list, but I couldn't tell that it was "the one", so I pressed hint. When everything is listed, nothing is pointed at.

**3. Hint and undo: what happened right after?**
- Hint on J-16: "Take back the lantern at A1 (1 of 1)." I tapped A1 off, and within a few seconds I saw that A2 lights the whole second row. Then C1 finished it. Same as last week: it says what, not why, but the why was easy to see afterwards.
- Undo on J-12 (D5) and J-20 (A2): the board went back cleanly and the stuck or red mark disappeared. No problem.

## Out of character: tester notes

- I played from the ASCII board and worked out the deductions as text. The session timestamps (about 25 seconds to 4 minutes per level) are much faster than a real phone player's. J-30 (8×8) would take a real Yuki several minutes.
- I chose my mistakes as plausible human first tries and knew in advance that they would fail: J-12 D5, J-16 A1, J-20 A2 (a long-row conflict) and J-30 E2. I used the J-16 hint to test the hint and the long stuck list. A real Yuki might have found A2 herself.
- **Stuck-reason finding:** the light cause is now reported (good), but on J-16 the reason listed every contributing lantern and rock (`light from A1 F3 D4 B6 E6; rocks G1 C3 G3`), so the one actionable culprit (A1) is not singled out. On J-30, `C3 (light from E2; rocks C2 A4 B4)` left out D4's light and rock D2 as contributors to D3 being blocked, but it still named the actionable lantern. So the list is sometimes complete-but-noisy and sometimes partial.
- **W-4 is identical to round 3's W-4.** It was solved from memory, so it gives no new difficulty signal.
- **Difficulty curve:** 12 (6×6, 9 lanterns) ≈ 16 (7×7, 9) < 20 (7×7, 11, sparse clues, long sightlines) < 30 (8×8, 13, many 0s). It rises monotonically, unlike rounds 2 and 3.
- **Screenshots:** In 05, the overfull mark uses "^^" chevrons, which read as "needs more". In 06, the remove-hint has no causal thread (unlike round 3's arrow to the 0). In 09, the flame count is 5 while the sample share text says 🔥3. That's probably just example data, but noted. The two small lanterns beside the streak in 09 are unexplained. The share sheet in 10 is in the device's Korean system locale, which is not part of the app.
- The judgments about her English comprehension and first-timer clarity are estimates from static PNGs.
- I opened only the allowed files and ran only `npm run -s play`. The project's CLAUDE.md was auto-loaded into my context and I ignored it.
