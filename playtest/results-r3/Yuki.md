**1. What I did, in order.**
On each J level I looked first for numbers whose free sides are exactly enough (the 2 next to a rock, the 3 next to a 0, the 2 against the edge) and placed those lanterns. Then I followed the 1s and the lit lines, and last I filled the lonely corners. On level 8 I made one wrong corner choice and undid it. On level 15 I chose the wrong pair at a 2, saw two stuck cells, and moved one lantern. No hints, no restarts.

**2. A moment I wasn't sure?**
Level 8, the top-left corner: A1 and B1 both looked good. Level 15, the 2 on the right with three free sides. Both times the board told me quickly.

**3. Hint?**
No hint on J.

**4. Stuck and couldn't tell why?**
A little, on level 15. The board said B3 was stuck "because of rocks A3, C3, A6". But in my head the real reason was my lantern at E1: its light ran along the top row and covered B1, the only good place for B3. The rocks were only half the story. I found it myself because I know Akari, but the reason it gave pointed the wrong way.

**5. Harder, easier, the same?**
It grew from 1 to 8 to 10, which was nice. Level 10 (7×7) was the hardest and the best. Then level 15 was 6×6 again and easier than 10, the same as last week. The top of J still doesn't feel like a "last level".

My daughter, who doesn't know Akari, would likely miss that rule until she saw the red conflict in the game. Also, nothing says "light every square", but I think the lit-up house and the empty dark squares make that guessable.

**10. How close to an app I'd keep on my home screen for months?**

## Out of character: tester notes

- I played from the ASCII board and worked out the deductions as text, with cell names. My session timestamps (13–29 seconds per level) are far faster than a human's. A real Yuki would take several minutes on J-10 (7×7).
- I chose my mistakes as plausible human first tries, and I knew in advance that they would fail: J-8 A1, J-15 E1 (with E3), W-4 A5. I used the W-4 hint deliberately, to test the hint. Without the guide notes a real Yuki might have solved W-4 herself. J-1, J-10 and W-1 were played straight because every step was forced.
- **Finding: the stuck reasons miss the light cause.** In J-15, `Stuck: B3 (rocks A3 C3 A6)`, the only remaining good cell (B1) was blocked by the light from my E1 lantern, but the reason listed only rocks. In W-4, `Stuck: B6 (rock C6)`, the real blocker was A5's light covering A6 and B5. The legend implies one reason per cell (rock or light). Mixed causes show only the rock part. The phone's thread-to-rock marker (04) probably has the same blind spot.
- On J-15, A6's "1" (satisfied by A5) appears in the B3 reason. That is correct but non-obvious.
- **Difficulty:** J-10 (7×7, 11 lanterns) is harder than J-15 (6×6, 8 lanterns). Same non-monotonic ending as round 2. W-1 is now an easy forced chain, a clear improvement over round 2's W-1.
- **Screenshots:** In onboarding 02, the placed lanterns render unlit (white paper style, floor not lit), unlike 01. In onboarding 01c, the conflict shows as a neutral blue dotted line with no "forbidden" cue. The overfull state (05) uses a dark, desaturated stone with a ring, which can read as "inactive". The left HUD button differs between gentle (path + lantern + "+") and deduce (house), and its purpose is not inferable. I did not see a completion/next screen this round.
- My judgments of her English comprehension and of first-timer clarity are estimates. I can read everything, and I see static PNGs, not a live device.
- I opened only the allowed files (tools/PLAYTEST.md, my two earlier result files, the listed screenshots) and ran only `npm run -s play`. Project CLAUDE.md was auto-loaded into context, and I ignored it.
