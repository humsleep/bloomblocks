# Playtest results: Yuki

**Persona:** Yuki, 45, Osaka (Japan). Part-time office worker. Plays on the 15–20 minute train commute and before bed. Plays nonogram apps and Meowdoku. Grew up doing Nikoli puzzles in magazines, so she already knows "Akari / Light Up" (black cells with numbers). Reads only basic English. Likes calm, beautiful design and dislikes clutter and loud ads.
**Play order:** V, then S, then C. Session files are in `playtest/sessions/Yuki/`.

## Version V (stones with numbers, no lantern limit)

Rule line: "Light every cell. Lanterns can't see each other. A number on a rock = lanterns touching it."

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 (5×5) | Completed | 6 | 0 | 0 | 0 | "Ah, this is Akari!" The 2 at the bottom fixed two lanterns at once, and after that it was one step at a time. Very pleasant. |
| 3 (5×5) | Completed | 6 | 0 | 0 | 0 | The same "2 on the bottom row" shape as level 1, so I knew the start straight away. The 0 next to the 1 on the left made it neat. Slightly too similar to level 1. |

**1. What I did, in order.**
I opened level 1 and saw numbers on the rocks, so I knew it was Akari. I always start from the big numbers and the 0s. On every level I placed the sure lanterns first (the 2 at the bottom on levels 1 and 3, the 3 on levels 8 and 15). Then I followed the numbers around them and filled the last dark corners. On level 8 I moved one lantern at the 2 in the top right. On level 15 I undid one lantern at the 3 in the top right. I finished all four without hints.

**2. A moment I wasn't sure?**
On level 8, at the "2" in the top right: there were three possible cells and I picked the wrong pair. On level 15 I briefly wasn't sure whether the 3's third lantern went above it or to the left. Both times the board showed me a dark cell that could not be lit any more, so I understood. That doesn't feel like confusion. It is the normal pleasure of thinking.

**3. One sentence: the next level?**
Find the biggest number and the 0s, put down the lanterns they force, then follow the lit lines to the remaining dark corners.

**4. Last level vs first?**
Harder, but in a nice way. Level 15 was bigger and had more numbers that work together, so I had to hold two or three ideas at once, but every step had a reason. Levels 1 and 3 felt almost the same (same opening).

## Version S (no numbers, a tight lantern limit)

Rule line: "Light every cell. Lanterns can't see each other. Use at most the shown lanterns."

| Level | Outcome | Moves | Undos | Hints | Restarts | How it felt |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 (5×5, max 6) | Completed with 5/6 | 5 | 0 | 0 | 0 | Easy. I just put lanterns where long lines are. Not much thinking. |
| 3 (5×5, max 4) | Completed with 4/4 | 4 | 0 | 0 | 0 | Only 4 lanterns, so I had to think a little about which lantern lights the most. Exactly 4, a small "phew". |
| 8 (6×6, max 6) | Completed with 6/6 | 10 | 0 | 0 | 1 | My first 4 lanterns lit most of the board, but then three separate dark places were left and only 2 lanterns. I cleared everything and started again. The second time I thought about the lonely cells first. It worked, but I was not sure why my first try was "wrong". It felt like counting, not logic. |
| 15 (7×7, max 6) | Completed with 6/6 | 10 | 0 | 0 | 1 | Same as level 8: my first try ran out of lanterns. Then I noticed the three long rows and the left and right columns each need a lantern, and one lantern in a corner of a long row can cover two of them. When I saw that, it was quite satisfying. But I had to restart to get there. |

**1. What I did, in order.**
Level 1: I placed 5 lanterns on long lines and finished under the limit. Level 3: I placed 4 with some care and used them all. Level 8: I placed 4, saw I could not finish with 2 more, restarted, and solved it with 6. Level 15: the same thing. I made a wrong start, restarted, thought about which rows and columns must each have a lantern, and solved it with exactly 6.

**2. A moment I wasn't sure?**
Yes. On level 8 and level 15, after my first few lanterns. There are no numbers, so the board doesn't tell me where to begin. You only find out you were wrong at the end, when the lanterns run out. Then I have to start again from nothing, and that is a little tiring.

**3. One sentence: the next level?**
Look for the cells that only one row or one column can reach and cover those first with as few lanterns as possible. If I run out, restart.

**4. Last level vs first?**
Much harder. Level 1 was nearly free. Level 15 needed a full plan, because one wrong lantern at the start ruins it and you only learn that at the end. It feels harder because of guessing and restarting, not because of more clever steps.

**1. What I did, in order.**
On every level I put lanterns on the long open rows and columns, then filled the dark gaps one by one. I never needed undo or restart, and I always had lanterns left over.

**2. A moment I wasn't sure?**
Not really. Only at the very start: "Is this the same as the last one (S)?" The rule text was the same. After that, never.

**3. One sentence: the next level?**
Put lanterns on the long lines, then fill whatever is still dark. The limit is big enough not to worry.

**4. Last level vs first?**
About the same. The board is bigger, so it takes a few more taps, but there is no real thinking. The limit is so generous that it never matters.

## After all three versions

**5. If I closed the app right now, what would I do instead?**
On the train I'd open my nonogram app and do one of the medium puzzles. Before bed maybe one Meowdoku. Or just look out the window.

**6. Tomorrow morning, 10 minutes. Choose ONE.**
**Version V.** It is real Akari, the kind I grew up with. Each level fits in a few minutes and every lantern has a reason, so I can stop at a station and still feel I finished something. Not Wordle: it is English words, too hard for me. Not Royal Match: too loud and flashy, and full of ads and pressure. Not S: I don't want to restart a level twice on a crowded train. Not C: it is too easy, I'd be bored before my stop. Not "don't touch the phone": 10 quiet minutes with a good puzzle is my small luxury.

**7. What would make me delete this app?**
Loud video ads between levels, or a timer and "hurry" pop-ups. Also if every level is like C (no thinking), or like S, where I must restart again and again without knowing why. And if important things are only explained in English sentences I can't read.

**8. Describe it to a friend in one sentence.**
「ニコリの美術館（Akari）みたいなパズルで、夜の庭に灯籠を置いて全部明るくするの。静かで落ち着くよ。」(It's like Nikoli's Akari: you put lanterns in a dark garden to light it all up. It's quiet and calming.)

**9. Ranking (most likely to play again → least).**
1. **V**: the numbers make each step logical, the way I know from Nikoli. It's satisfying, and I never felt cheated.
2. **S**: the last level had a clever idea, but finding it meant restarting, and with no numbers I couldn't see where to begin.
3. **C**: pretty, but the lantern limit never matters, so there's nothing to solve.

**10. The current-look screenshots.**
What I notice first: it's very dark navy and calm, which I like. No ads and no clutter. The yellow lantern circles are pretty, like small paper lanterns. The red pair in the conflict picture is clear immediately, even without reading.
Hard to see: the stones (lighter gray-blue squares) and the dark floor (dark navy squares) are close in color. On a bright train or with tired eyes at night, I'd have to look twice to see which squares are rocks. The rocks also look "raised", like buttons I could press. The lit color is a brownish mustard, not as warm as I'd expect for "light". There's a lot of empty space above the board, and on level 15 the board fills the whole width but sits low near my thumb. Fine for one hand, but the top half of the screen is just empty.
English: "Light every tile", "Two lanterns see each other", "Garden lit!", "Next". "Next" and "Level" I understand. "Two lanterns see each other" I could guess from the red color, not from the words. The six small circles under the title: I guessed they are the lanterns I can use, because they turned yellow one by one, but nothing says so. The level-15 picture has no numbers on the rocks, so I'd miss my Akari numbers.
Would I keep it on my phone? Yes, it's calm and beautiful enough. But I would want the stones easier to tell apart, and the numbers (V style).

**11. The full-game idea (hill town, one daily puzzle, shareable result).**
The town lighting up house by house at night is lovely. I can picture my commute adding one window each day, and that's a gentle reason to come back that doesn't pressure me. The shared daily puzzle is also nice. My daughter or a coworker could do the same one and we could compare, as long as the result is simple marks and not English text. What would bring me back on day 7 is not the sharing, really. It's (a) puzzles still having a real logical "aha" like V level 15, not easy filler, and (b) seeing my little town clearly change: a week of lit windows, maybe a small seasonal touch like a festival lantern. If the daily puzzle were C-style easy, I'd stop by day 3 even with the pretty town.

## Out of character: tester notes

- I read the ASCII grid and worked out the deductions as text (by cell coordinates). For V, that is roughly how a Nikoli solver works, but a real person on a small phone screen would be slower at tracking long row and column sightlines, especially on 7×7. My solve times in the session logs (about 15–50 seconds) are far faster than a real human's. I worked out most moves before running the commands, so the logged timestamps do not reflect thinking time.
- My mistakes were partly chosen to be realistic. On V-8, V-15, S-8 and S-15 I played a plausible wrong first attempt (removing F1, the F1 undo, and the two restarts) after judging that a human would likely start that way. In S-8 and S-15 the first attempts really were what an untrained person would try first, but I knew they would fail before I placed them. A real human might need more than one restart, or give up, on S-15. I also never used a hint, while a real casual player might have used one on S-8 and S-15.
- I have no time pressure, no train jostling and no fatigue, which are the main real-world factors for this persona.
- For C, I chose not to minimize lanterns (I just covered the board), which I believe matches a casual player. The finding that "the limit never bound" is robust: C's limits were 2, 2, 1 and 1 above what I used.
- For the screenshots, my comment that the stones and the dark floor are close in color is based on the rendered PNGs. Real perceived contrast depends on the device's brightness and glare.
- The persona's limited English is simulated. I can read all the text, so my judgment of which phrases she'd understand is an estimate.
