**Persona:** Sophie, 47, Brisbane. Hospital nurse on rotating shifts. Plays in 2–3 minute breaks and on the bus (Block Blast, Vita Mahjong). Needs reading glasses; small or low-contrast things annoy her. Often interrupted. Wants to switch off, not be tested. Drops a game if she gets stuck twice.

Play order: S, then C, then V. Sessions: `playtest/sessions/Sophie/<code>-<level>.json`.

## Final answers (in character)

**5. If you closed the app right now, what would you do instead?**
Open Block Blast for a round, or just look out the bus window. I wouldn't go looking for another puzzle; my head's had enough.

**6. Tomorrow morning, 10 minutes. ONE of [Wordle, Royal Match, S, V, C, don't touch the phone]?**
Version C. It's the one where I never felt stupid. Plenty of lanterns, a cosy little "garden lit" at the end, and if the ward calls me I can pick it up again without having to remember a plan. Not Wordle, because it's one go a day and I'm usually doing it with my daughter in the evening anyway. Not Royal Match, because it's loud and keeps trying to sell me things and I lose 30 minutes. Not S, because it's nearly the same but that tight limit on the big boards gives me the "you've already lost and don't know it yet" feeling. Not V, because that's homework. Not "don't touch the phone": 10 minutes in the morning is exactly what I'd use a gentle game for.

**7. What would make you delete this app?**
Getting stuck two levels in a row with no clear way out, like V level 15. Also if the stones and the dark squares stay the same murky blue, so I have to put my glasses on to tell them apart. Ads between every level would do it too.

**8. Describe it to a friend in one sentence.**
"You put little lanterns on a dark garden grid so every square lights up, but two lanterns can't see each other. It's calming when it's not trying to be too clever."

**9. Rank S, V, C (most likely to play again to least):**
1. **C**: Same nice idea as S but with a spare lantern, so I could switch off and still win.
2. **S**: Fine, but the tight limit on the big level made me need a hint, and I only found my mistake at the very end.
3. **V**: The numbers turned it into maths. One stuck level and one give-up out of four, and that's my limit.

**10. The current-look screenshots:**
- First thing I notice: it's very dark. Navy on navy. The top half of the screen is just empty dark space, and the grid sits at the bottom near my thumb (good for the bus).
- Hard to see: the stones (lighter grey-blue) and the dark floor squares (dark navy) are close in colour. On the 7×7 one at arm's length without glasses I had to squint to count the stones in the middle row. The little hollow circles under "Level 1" are tiny and faint; I only worked out they were the lantern count when they turned yellow on the conflict screen. "Light every tile" is in a small, pale grey.
- What works: the red "Two lanterns see each other" with the red-outlined squares is crystal clear. The yellow lanterns pop nicely. The "Garden lit!" screen with the big yellow Next button is lovely and easy to hit.
- The lit floor is a muddy brownish-mustard. I expected warm glowing light and it looks more like cardboard.
- Would I keep it on my phone? Maybe. It's calm, and that's what I want, but I'd want the stones and dark squares easier to tell apart and the lit squares to feel warmer.

**11. The full game (town lights up, daily shared puzzle, paste-able result):**
The little town lighting up house by house is the part I like. That's a reason to come back that isn't "beat a harder level": I'd want to see my town get brighter, the way I like finishing a Vita Mahjong picture. The daily puzzle shared with everyone is OK, but only if it's a gentle one. If the daily is a V-style number puzzle and I fail it in front of my group chat, I won't be pasting anything. On day 7, what would bring me back is that the town is half lit and I want to see it finished, and that every day's puzzle can be done in one bus stop without needing a hint.

## Out of character: tester notes

- **I solved faster and more cleanly than a real Sophie probably would.** On S and C I mostly worked out a full solution in my head before tapping, so those levels show zero undos and very few moves. A real 47-year-old after a shift would likely tap, look, undo and adjust a few times, especially on S-8 and C-8 where the budget was exactly used up. Treat the S/C move counts as a lower bound on effort.
- **On S-15, V-8 and V-15 I deliberately played "intuitive first taps"** (middle first; three lanterns around the 3) rather than deducing first, to mimic her style. The wrong moves, conflicts, hints and the V-15 give-up came from those choices. The give-up decision applied the persona rule ("stuck twice"). In V-15 I could see the fix (move D1 to C1 and add D7), but Sophie plausibly would not.
- **The ASCII grid is easier for me than a phone screen would be for her.** Cell names, the exact `Lit x/y`, and messages like "too many lanterns at A6" are very explicit. On a phone she'd have to read the numbers on low-contrast stones herself. The screenshots suggest she'd struggle to tell stone from dark floor, which the text board hides completely.
- **Comparing versions:** S and C share the same rule text, and C's budgets were looser (spares on 3 of 4 levels). V has no budget but number constraints, and its hard levels need long-range deduction, which my spatial reasoning handles more easily than a casual player would.
- **Times in the logs are not human times.** Wall-clock seconds reflect my command batching, not thinking time, and there were no real interruptions.
- **Unreliable message:** on V-8 the hint named a cell (D1) that immediately conflicted with my existing lantern. A real player could read that as the hint being wrong.
